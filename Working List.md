Great question — a **Noble House** doesn’t need as much granularity as a military or religious faction, but it does need to capture its **identity, influence, and legacy** in a way that makes it playable and story-rich. Here’s a streamlined template I suggest for your Noble Houses (I’ll keep it flexible so you can apply it across humans, elves, dwarves, etc.):

---

# 🏰 Noble House Template

## 1. **Basic Information**

- **Name:**
    
- **Symbol/Heraldry:**
    
- **Seat / Domain:** (Where they rule from)
    
- **Current Head:** (King, Duke, Matriarch, etc.)
    
- **Core Traits:** (e.g., honour-bound, cunning, isolationist, mercantile)
    

---

## 2. **Overview**

A concise summary of the house: who they are, what they represent, and how they are perceived in the wider world.

---

## 3. **History**

- **Origins:** How/when they rose to prominence.
    
- **Notable Events:** Wars, alliances, betrayals, marriages, or turning points that defined their legacy.
    
- **Current Status:** Where the house stands today (prosperous, declining, suspicious, etc.).
    

---

## 4. **Leadership & Family**

- **Current Leader(s):** Who rules the house.
    
- **Notable Members:** Key figures past or present (war heroes, traitors, scholars, eccentrics).
    
- **Cadet Branches:** (Optional) Splinter lines or relatives who hold secondary titles.
    

---

## 5. **Culture & Reputation**

How the house is viewed internally (values, traditions, quirks) and externally (their reputation with other nobles, commoners, or foreign powers).

---

## 6. **Territory & Influence**

- **Primary Seat:** Their ancestral castle, city, or capital.
    
- **Wider Holdings:** Villages, lands, or resources they control.
    
- **Influence:** Economic (trade, mining, shipping), political (alliances, court positions), or cultural (patrons of the arts, religious sponsors, etc.).
    

---

## 7. **Alliances & Rivalries**

- **Allies:** Houses or factions they align with.
    
- **Rivals/Enemies:** Who opposes or distrusts them.
