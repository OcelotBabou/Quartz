### 1. **Overview**

Orestia is the southernmost realm of the Shattered Kingdoms, often asserting its distinction from the fractured region by claiming that the Shattered Kingdoms truly begin north of the Mesoramus Range. This self-perception has led Orestia to actively avoid the internal conflicts and power struggles that plague its northern neighbors, though it frequently finds itself entangled in the schemes and ambitions of Mesokini, its closest neighbor. The kingdom’s terrain is dominated by expansive, semi-fertile plains that rely heavily on the winter runoff from the Mesoramus Range to sustain agriculture and livestock, making its fortunes closely tied to the land’s delicate balance.

### 2. **Geography**

Orestia is an enclosed kingdom defined by natural barriers on nearly all sides. To the southeast lie the Collis Brigantium, bandit-infested hills that present a constant threat to travelers and trade routes. The Mesoramus Range forms its northeastern boundary, providing a rugged, mountainous backdrop and a vital source of water. Wrapping around from the west to the southwest is the Arteria River, a sprawling waterway that acts as both a natural border and a critical artery for transport and trade.

The kingdom itself is largely composed of flat plains crisscrossed by meandering tributaries that feed into the Arteria River. These plains, while fertile during wetter seasons, are prone to the harsh summer heat and droughts typical of central Siar’Morrin. The lifeblood of Orestia’s agriculture comes from the Mesoramus Range, where winter runoff in spring rejuvenates the land, briefly transforming the plains into a verdant and productive landscape.

### 3. **History**

Before the infestation of the Collis Brigantium by bandits and the schism that fractured the Shattered Kingdoms, Orestia flourished as a vital link between the inner kingdoms of Siar’Morrin and New Birania. Its position along key trade routes made it a prosperous and bustling kingdom, serving as the first point of contact for merchants and travelers moving between these regions.

However, the rise of banditry in the Collis Brigantium severed these crucial connections, cutting Orestia off from New Birania and isolating it from the larger trade networks. As the Shattered Kingdoms descended into chaos and infighting, Orestia’s once-prominent role diminished, leaving its economy in tatters.

Today, Orestia is a kingdom divided. One faction, bitter over perceived abandonment by New Birania, advocates aligning with Mesokini, seeing this as their best chance for survival and stability within the Shattered Kingdoms. Others, clinging to the hope of renewal, seek to reestablish ties with New Birania, envisioning a future where Orestia can rise above the scheming and turmoil that define its northern neighbors. This internal division has left the kingdom in a state of stagnation, teetering between its past prosperity and an uncertain future.

### 4. **Political Structure**

Orestia is governed by a monarchy, though its current ruler, King [[Lothar Evrard]], has become a figure of contention. At only 43 years old, Lothar suffers from severe illness, leaving his reign marked by indecision and perceived weakness. His inability to resolve the critical debate over aligning with either New Birania or Mesokini has fueled widespread dissatisfaction, with many blaming him for Orestia’s faltering state.

This division is further exacerbated by the conflict among his five children, each representing opposing visions for the kingdom’s future. The eldest, Uthar, and his third-born, Dalfin, advocate aligning with Mesokini, viewing it as a pragmatic move to secure Orestia’s survival within the fractured Shattered Kingdoms. Conversely, Enna, Clodar and Gisla strongly support reestablishing ties with New Birania, hoping to restore Orestia’s former prosperity by distancing the kingdom from the turmoil of its northern neighbors.

With King Lothar’s health rapidly declining, fears of an impending power struggle between the siblings grow stronger. Many worry that this familial conflict could plunge Orestia further into instability, leaving it vulnerable to external pressures and internal collapse.

### 5. **Economy**

Orestia’s economy once thrived on trade, serving as a critical hub between New Birania and the inner kingdoms of Siar’Morrin. However, the severing of trade routes due to the rise of banditry in the Collis Brigantium and its isolation within the Shattered Kingdoms have left the kingdom economically stagnant.

Today, Orestia functions primarily as a self-sustaining region, relying on agriculture and the remnants of its once-vast coffers to support its people. While the fertile plains and seasonal runoff from the Mesoramus Range provide enough resources to sustain the population, the lack of trade and external investment has left the kingdom struggling to maintain infrastructure and adapt to changing times. This reliance on old wealth and subsistence agriculture has fostered an air of resignation among its people, even as factions within the kingdom argue over how to reclaim economic vitality.

### 6. **Culture and Society**

The culture of Orestia reflects its dual identity as both a part of the Shattered Kingdoms and a realm striving to distance itself from their chaos. Its people are deeply traditional, holding fast to customs that date back to its prosperous trading days, yet there is a growing sense of division as the kingdom grapples with its uncertain future.

Orestians are proud of their agricultural heritage, with festivals celebrating the spring runoff and harvest season forming the backbone of communal life. These events often include feasts, music, and storytelling, detailing myths about the Mesoramus Range and the Arteria River.

The kingdom's isolation has fostered a strong sense of self-reliance among its people. Orestians are resilient and resourceful, often turning to local craftsmanship and ingenuity to make up for the lack of imported goods. Blacksmithing, weaving, and pottery thrive as essential trades, with artisans passing their skills down through generations. However, this insularity has also bred a degree of mistrust toward outsiders.

The political divide within the monarchy has also seeped into everyday life. Town squares and markets buzz with debates over the kingdom’s future, with loyalties to New Birania or Mesokini shaping social interactions and even friendships. This tension is most palpable in the kingdom’s larger settlements, where public forums often devolve into heated arguments.

Religion in Orestia is predominantly tied to the worship of Statera and the Church of Statera, but with a uniquely local twist. The Arteria River and Mesoramus Range are often venerated as sacred manifestations of balance and life. Small shrines dot the countryside, dedicated to these natural landmarks and the gods believed to guide them. In recent years, however, whispers of older, more regional deities have begun to resurface, particularly in the isolated corners of the kingdom.

### 7. **Military**

Orestia’s military is a shadow of its former strength, having suffered devastating losses during the **Schism Wars**. Once a proud and formidable force, the kingdom’s army has dwindled to a modest contingent that struggles to maintain order within its borders.

The majority of Orestia’s remaining forces are now concentrated along the southeastern boundaries, where they face the constant threat posed by the bandit-infested Collis Brigantium. These troops are primarily engaged in skirmishes to protect bordering villages and prevent the bandits from gaining a foothold deeper into Orestian territory. Despite their best efforts, the sheer number of bandits and the challenging terrain often leave the soldiers stretched thin.

The military's equipment and training have also suffered due to the kingdom’s economic decline. Without the resources to properly arm and maintain a standing force, many soldiers rely on outdated weapons and makeshift repairs. Morale remains a significant issue, as the troops are often overworked and underpaid, their sacrifices met with limited recognition or reward.
