Shortly after, Bradbury returns to the group, his usual jovial demeanour tinged with seriousness. He announces that Lieve has discovered a small band of bandits encircling a nearby farmstead, likely planning a raid or worse. He discusses his plan to return with Lieve to offer what aid he can to the farmstead’s inhabitants. Thorton, however, disputes Bradbury's suggestion to leave the caravan. He argues that with the threat of bandits close by their leader's place is with the caravan to ensure everyone's safety. Acknowledging this valid point, Bradbury proposes an alternative: he asks if any of the players or other caravan members would be willing to go in his stead to help Lieve assess the situation and possibly aid the farmstead, promising recompense.

Bradbury assures those who volunteer that he will manage the caravan's defences in their absence and provides them with necessary supplies and a quick briefing on what Lieve observed.

**The Farmstead**

_As twilight deepens, the eerie silence of the forest accompanies your cautious march, guided by Lieve's shadowy figure. He moves with such stealth and precision that it would be easy to lose sight of him if not for his frequent pauses, ensuring no one is left behind. After about forty-five minutes of tense navigation, Lieve's pace slows, and he gestures for silence, indicating you are nearing your destination._

_Peering through the dimming light, you catch the first signs of trouble. Wisps of smoke curl into the sky, and as you edge closer to the forest’s boundary, the full scene unfolds before you. A farmhouse stands engulfed in chaos; one of its outer haystacks is fiercely ablaze, casting a harsh light over the scene. Six bandits are scattered around the yard—four of them laugh raucously, slapping their thighs and jesting at the flames, while two others frantically toss more hay against the building’s side, fuelling the fire that threatens to consume the structure. The crackling of fire mixes with their cruel laughter, setting a grim backdrop for your arrival._

Allow players to decide their approach. If they choose stealth, the DC is only 8 as the bandits are distracted by the fire.

**Bandit Engagement Dynamics**

- **Initial Engagement:** The four mocking bandits are likely to be caught off guard and can be surprised by the party. They are primarily focused on taunting and have lower guard against an attack.
- **Reaction of Fuel Gatherers:** The two bandits gathering additional fuel are initially more focused on their task but will quickly realize the threat once combat starts. After a round or two, depending on how the combat unfolds, they will join the fight to support their comrades.
- **Bandit Tactics:** The bandits are unorganized and react primarily to immediate threats. If they are outnumbered or sustain injuries early in the fight, they are likely to try to disengage and escape into the surrounding forest.

**Capturing a Bandit**

- **Interaction with Captured Bandit:** If the party manages to capture one of the bandits alive, he will initially be belligerent, especially towards any non-human party members. His demeanour can be hostile, and he might refuse to talk unless coerced or persuaded.
- **Information Extraction:** With the right approach (intimidation, persuasion, or other means), the captured bandit reveals that his group is part of a larger band. They have been exploiting the increased traffic due to the upcoming festival, particularly targeting elves and merchants. He shares that they attacked the farmstead after being repeatedly denied food and supplies they demanded for their camp.
- **Reason for Hostility:** The bandit explains their particular disdain for elves and hints that their leader was tipped off by an informant in Hu Dao about the festival, presenting a lucrative opportunity for ambushes.

**Farmstead Aftermath**

**Scenario Introduction**

As the last of the bandits flees into the thickening darkness or is subdued, the party's attention turns to the farmhouse. The condition of the building and the well-being of its inhabitants depend heavily on how quickly and effectively the party intervened during the attack.

**Describing the Scene**

- **If the Farmhouse is Severely Damaged:** Describe the smouldering remains of parts of the structure, with evident structural damage that poses a risk of collapse. The air is thick with the smell of smoke, and small fires may still need extinguishing.
- **If the Farmhouse is Largely Intact:** The building has survived with moderate damage, such as broken windows or scorched walls, but it remains habitable.

**Interaction with the Inhabitants**

- **Initial Encounter:** As the party approaches, they meet two elderly humans, visibly shaken but unharmed. They express profound relief at the sight of the party, overwhelmed by the recent threat yet grateful for the intervention. They will introduce themselves as Jack and Jill.
- **Dialogue and Reaction:**

- **Gratitude and Offer:** Regardless of their financial state, the couple offers what little they have—food and possibly shelter. "We don’t have much, but what’s ours is yours for the night, should you need it," Jack says,   gesturing towards the modest farmhouse.
- **Request for Assistance:** If the house is severely damaged, they hesitantly ask if they might accompany the group to Hu Dao, hoping to find aid in rebuilding their lives. "We’ve nowhere else to turn, and Hu Dao’s our best chance at starting over," they explain with a hopeful yet anxious tone.

- **Non-Human Dynamics:** The elderly couple is visibly uncomfortable around any non-human members of the party. They subtly but clearly gravitate towards any human members, thanking them more profusely and engaging them more directly in conversation.

- **If There Are No Human Members or Hostility is Shown:** The couple's demeanour becomes more reserved. They limit their offer to food and refrain from asking to join the party to Hu Dao. Their interactions are polite but distant, and they avoid further engagement beyond necessary hospitality.
