In the boundless void before time measured the cosmos, there existed only the incorporeals, beings of pure energy so vast and powerful that their very essence was woven into the fabric of the universe. These beings were locked in an eternal war, a relentless battle that had neither beginning nor end. Their names were lost to the chaos, their forms ever-shifting and indistinct, but their might was undeniable and terrifying to behold.

These divine beings clashed across the void in battles that illuminated the darkness with bursts of ethereal light, each confrontation a cataclysm that echoed through the nothingness. The forces involved were monumental, drawing on the raw energies of creation itself. Each being wielded powers that could shape or shatter realms, yet no single force could dominate, for each time a god fell, it was reborn elsewhere—a cycle as constant as it was futile.

This cycle of death and rebirth was not without consequence. The energies expended in their endless conflicts were so immense that not all could be contained. With each clash, minute amounts of this celestial power escaped, seeping away from the ethereal into the nascent whispers of the material plane. Over ages beyond comprehension, these remnants of raw energy began to accumulate, not merely as scattered sparks, but as the seeds of matter itself.

Slowly, imperceptibly, this matter began to coalesce. Driven by the residual essence of the gods’ battles, these specks of materiality grew denser and more complex, aggregating under the unseen forces of what would one day be recognized as Quawerah, the God of all matter, the material universe given sentience. What began as rarefied particles drifted together, forming the first crude physical substances in a universe that had known only energy.

This accumulation of matter was the silent testimony to the gods' relentless warfare, a sign that even in their immortality, their endless duels were not without cost. The energies that had once been wielded so freely were now giving birth to something new, something unintended by the incorporeals—matter, the foundation of a material plane that would one day host life and stories far removed from the endless conflicts that had inadvertently birthed it.

Thus, the stage was set, not by design but by accident, for the emergence of Quawerah, the being that would come to embody the essence of this nascent material universe. From the forgotten echoes of the gods’ clashes, a new form of existence was taking shape, one that would herald the dawn of creation and challenge the eternal cycle of conflict that had spawned it.
