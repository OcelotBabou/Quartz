**Overview:** 

Stretching along the westward side of Pilliga, the Badlands are a parched and craggy expanse where the land itself seems to reject comfort. Blasted by dust storms and broken by jagged ravines, this desolate region is far from the trade routes and too barren to tempt settlers. Instead, it has become a refuge for the unwanted — thieves, exiles, and worse — carving out survival in the shadows of fractured stone.

**Description:** 

The Badlands are a sprawl of sun-scorched rock and shifting grit. Its ravines twist like scar tissue across the land, some so narrow that only a lone rider could pass through, others deep enough to swallow sound entirely. Vegetation is sparse, scattered in stubborn clumps, and water is nearly nonexistent outside the rarest of condensation points.

Yet within these rugged veins, life clings on — crude but clever. Over generations, those who’ve made the Badlands their home have learned to survive through ingenuity and desperation. Underground bunkers are cooled by carved air tunnels. Water condensers, scavenged or home-built, collect what precious moisture the sky offers. Homes are hidden beneath rock or blended into canyon walls, invisible until you’re too close to leave.

Some here trade in salvage or information. Others subsist in isolation. But many survive through raiding — striking at weak convoys, foraging into Pilliga’s outposts, and battling rival bands over every precious resource. Pilliga may not be rich, but to the Badlanders, even scarcity is worth fighting for.

**History:** 

For most of recorded history, the Badlands have been a footnote — too barren to cultivate, too remote to conquer, and too treacherous to map. During the Age of Chaos, when demons tore through more prosperous lands, the Badlands were largely left alone — too sparse, too dry, too unworthy of effort. It was here that some found refuge, willingly or not. Outcasts, fugitives, and wanderers settled in the shadows, carving lives from dust and stone.

For others, their arrival is more recent — driven into the region by political exile, criminal pasts, or foul schemes better fled than fought. Yet over generations, many who came to survive remained to belong. For some, the Badlands are no longer a refuge — they are home. A hard-won homeland where the outside world is viewed with suspicion, even contempt. Among these hardened families and self-made clans, survival has become culture. The Badlanders are territorial, fiercely independent, and expert in wringing life from the most unyielding of landscapes. To them, the idea of returning to “civilisation” is not only foreign — it is absurd.

**Significance:** 

Westrun’s value lies not in its culture or its politics, but in its function. It is the industrial heart of Pilliga’s river trade — a cornerstone of the region’s economic lifeblood. Every barge that travels the Fluvitae, every shipment that crosses from East to West, passes through the city’s docks or ledgers. Its shipwrights are considered some of the best in eastern Siar’Morrin, and its warehouses supply settlements as far afield as Agroma’s borderlands. Though it lacks the civil prestige of Eastwatch, Westrun holds no illusions — it doesn’t need praise. It builds the boats that carry it.

**Notable Features:**

- **Rust River:** A rare lifeline in a dying land, the Rust River cuts through the western Badlands, its red-stained waters bearing the iron-rich runoff from the [[Shadow Aisle Mountains]]. It is the only somewhat permanent surface water in the region, and because of this, it is fiercely contested. Raids and ambushes are common along its banks, and most Badlanders avoid it unless absolutely necessary — its water may sustain you, but your presence there rarely goes unnoticed.
- **Niphar’s Rest:** The largest canyon in the Badlands and a region unto itself, Niphar’s Rest stretches so vast and deep that entire communities dwell within its craggy folds — many unaware of each other’s existence. Its stone walls rise like sunbaked cathedrals, and its winding paths descend into darkness so thick it feels ancient. Trade, settlement, and conflict all occur within its shadowed depths, and even the most seasoned Badlanders speak of areas they've never dared to explore.
- **Xietem’s Web:** A maze of impossibly narrow ravines and interwoven crevices, Xietem’s Web is the stuff of cartographer nightmares and outlaw whispers. Claustrophobic and twisting, the passages coil around each other like a net — one wrong turn can strand a traveller for days. Strange echoes carry on the wind within, and some swear that whispers follow the lost until they forget which direction they came from.
- **Lepsis’ Causeway:** A narrow canyon pass that channels storm runoff into a sudden and deadly drop, Lepsis’ Causeway transforms during the regions rare rains into a violent cascade. Despite the danger, it remains a known gathering point, as water remains pooled at its base longer than most places in the Badlands. 

**Local Legends or Stories:** 

Though much of what is said about the Badlands walks the line between rumour and myth, one tale resurfaces more often than the rest. It is said that during the Age of Darkness ([[AoD]]), after the fall of Fort Logos, it is said that a great number of ork warbands broke from the main force and vanished into the Badlands. Familiar with harsh terrain and relentless survival, they slipped into the ravines and cracks of the land, evading pursuit. Some say they’ve built a hidden stronghold deep within the Badlands. Here the orks bide their time, and build their numbers, waiting for the perfect time to strike out again. 

**Current Status:** 

The Badlands remain largely unmapped and uncontrolled. Rumours of deep hideouts, hidden shrines, and darker, inhuman things beneath the rocks persist, but little is confirmed. What is known: the Badlands are no place for the unprepared — and once you go in, there’s no guarantee you’ll be coming out.
